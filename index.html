Privacy Policy

In effect from 13 October 2026

The short version

  • You never make an account, and the game never asks for your name, your email or your phone number.
  • The first time you open the game, it asks how old you are. That answer decides how your name is shown to other players: if you are under 13, other players see a nickname the game makes up for you, and only friends you have accepted see the name you chose.
  • The game gives your save file a random number when you first open it. That number is how the leaderboard tells one player from another. It is not your phone, and it is not you.
  • You get a cat name such as SleepyBiscuit. You can change it. Other players can see it (or, if you are under 13, your friends can), so please do not put your real name in it.
  • When you finish a match, the game sends the result of that match so it can appear on the leaderboard and so other people can play against a recording of your run.
  • You can add friends. A friend sees when you are online and can invite you to play. There is no typed chat: friends can only send each other a few fixed phrases, such as GOOD GAME!. On Android, the game can tell you when a friend comes online.
  • On Android there are ads, but only videos you choose to watch for a reward, from Google AdMob. They are never personalised: every ad request tells Google to treat the player as a child, and the game cannot read your phone's advertising ID. There are no ads on iPhone. How the ads work.
  • Nothing you do here is sold, shared for marketing, or used to follow you around other apps.
  • You can delete your account and everything our servers hold about it: in the game (Settings → YOUR ACCOUNT → DELETE ACCOUNT), or on this page if you no longer have the game. It happens 7 days after you ask, so it can be undone if you change your mind.

Who wrote this

What Meowtropolis never collects

This is not a promise about how we behave. These things simply are not in the game — there is no code anywhere that asks for them.

  • No name, email address or phone number. There is no sign-up form and no login screen.
  • No advertising ID and no hardware ID. The permission that lets an app read the advertising ID is removed from the game when it is built, so neither the game nor Google's ad library inside it can read it. Nothing lets anyone follow you into another company's app. (On Android, friend alerts need an address that lets a notification reach this game on your phone, explained below; and Google's ad library uses an “app set ID”, explained below.)
  • No location. The game has no permission to know where you are and never asks for it. It does read which country your phone is set to and its time-zone setting; here is why.
  • No contacts, photos, camera, microphone or files. Your friends in the game are people you added inside the game, not your phone's contacts.
  • No personalised ads and no tracking companies. The only ads are the reward videos from Google AdMob that you choose to watch, and every request for one is marked as coming from a child. There is no attribution service and no outside analytics service in the game, and Unity's own hardware statistics and engine diagnostics are switched off.
  • No typed chat. Players cannot write text to each other. Friends can send one of eight fixed phrases, and nothing else.
  • No card numbers or payment details. If you buy coins, Google handles the payment. The game never sees the card.
  • No birthday. The game asks how old you are once, and uses the answer only to decide how your name is shown to other players (see your age).

Who you are, inside the game

Your player number

The first time you open Meowtropolis, the game writes a long random number into its save file on your phone. Every match result the game sends carries that number, so the leaderboard knows that today's run and last week's run belong to the same player.

It is a fresh random number, not anything taken from your phone. Nobody can work backwards from it to a person, a device or an account. Deleting the game deletes the save file, and the link is genuinely broken — a new install starts again as somebody new.

One exception, and it is your choice: if you use the feature that links two of your own phones, you type the same short code on both, and that code becomes the player number on both of them.

Your friend code

To make friends possible, the game also gives you a friend code: eight random letters and numbers, such as K7M2QX9A. Like your player number, it is made up by the game and is not taken from you or your phone. It is what you give a friend so they can add you.

Friends are keyed to the account Unity gives your copy of the game (see playing against other people), not to your player number. That account number is never shown to anyone.

Your age

The first time you open Meowtropolis, it asks how old you are. The game asks this for one reason: to decide how your name is shown to other players (below). The answer does not change which ads you can see — every ad request is treated as coming from a child, whatever age is given (see Ads).

Your cat name

The game gives you a two-word cat name from a fixed list of cat words — SleepyBiscuit, GrumpyWaffle, and so on. You can roll a new one, or type your own instead.

If you are 13 or older, your name is public inside the game. It is printed next to your score on the leaderboards, it is shown to other players when they meet a recording of one of your runs, it is shown to the people you play live matches with, and your friends see it on their friends list. Please treat it as something the world can read, and do not type your real name, your school, or anything you would not want a stranger to see.

If you are under 13, other players never see the name you chose. Everywhere this page says other players see your cat name — leaderboards, recordings of your runs, live matches, friend requests — they see a nickname the game makes up for you instead. Only friends you have accepted see the name you chose.

A typed name has to be 3 to 14 letters or digits, with no spaces or symbols, and it is checked against a list of rude and hateful words on both the phone and the server. That check catches the obvious ones. It cannot catch everything, so if you ever see a name that should not be there, please tell us and it will be removed.

What the game sends when you play

After a match

When a match finishes, the game sends one small record of that match to our own server. It contains:

  • your player number and your cat name;
  • which map you played and which cat you wore;
  • twenty-five numbers describing how big your cat grew during the match;
  • which skills you had equipped and how many times you used each one;
  • a few numbers describing how you played — how much you chased, how far you roamed, how eagerly you used skills;
  • your rank badge, your total wins and your rating on that map.

Those last numbers exist for one reason: they let the game replay your run as an opponent for somebody else. When you play the online mode, some of the cats around you can be recordings of other people's matches, played back. Yours can become one of them.

The leaderboards

From the same match, the server keeps your best result on that map — your name, your cat, your score, your rating, your wins and your rank badge — so the leaderboard can be ordered. After a 1 v 1 duel, the game also sends your duel rating, your wins, your losses and your best winning streak, for the duel ladder.

When the game asks the server for a leaderboard, it sends your player number so that the server can point at your own row and tell you your position. The server never sends anybody's player number back to anybody. All you ever see about another player is the name, the cat and the numbers on their row.

How the game is used

Meowtropolis records what happens in the game itself, so it can be made better: which maps people stop choosing, which skills nobody buys, where in the tutorial people give up. These records carry your player number and, once the game has connected to Unity, your Unity account number (see below), so that a reinstall or a new phone counts as the same player. There is no free text of any kind in them.

Each batch of records also describes the copy of the game that sent it: the game's version, Android or iPhone, whether it was installed from Google Play, the country the phone is set to (taken from the SIM card, the mobile network or the phone's language setting — never a position), the phone's language, its model name, how much memory it has, how many hours ahead of or behind UTC it is, and when the game was first opened on it. This is so the numbers from real players can be told apart from our own test phones, and read by country and by kind of phone.

Your coins and what you own

So that coins cannot simply be made up by a changed copy of the game, our server keeps the count. After matches, gifts, videos and purchases, the game sends what you earned, spent and unlocked, and the server keeps your coins, fish, levels and everything you own, under your Unity account number (see below), together with your player number, a random number for each phone you play on, and the times of your recent matches, so it can check that the numbers are possible. The count on your phone is a copy of the server's.

Playing live against other people

The live modes — 1 v 1, the online city match, Catball and Cats vs Dogs — connect phones through Unity, the company whose software the game is built with. Unity provides the room where players meet and the connection their phones talk over.

Unity also gives every copy of the game an account of its own when the game starts, so that its services will answer. It has no name, email or phone number on it. It has a number, and it holds your friend code, your friends list (see Friends) and a copy of your save, so your progress can come back if you sign in on a new phone.

What the phones in a match tell each other is: your cat name, which cat you are wearing, your skills and their levels, your rating or rank, where your cat is moving and what it has eaten. In the online city match, the room also holds a number saying how well your phone can run the match, so the strongest phone can host it. Your player number is not sent. The other phones in the room can see your Unity account number, because that is how Unity's rooms work, and it is never shown to anyone.

To find players who are near you first, a waiting room shows other phones looking for a match how many hours ahead of or behind UTC the phone that opened it is — for example, “three hours ahead”. That is the time-zone setting on the phone, never your position. The country the phone is set to is used only in the usage records described above, so the numbers can be read country by country; it is never shown to other players.

After a live match, each player's game keeps a short “played with you” list on their own phone, the last 25 players, with their cat names and cats, so they can send a friend request. So the people you played with keep your cat name in that list, and you keep theirs. A friend request does nothing until it is accepted.

Friends

The friends list, friend requests, blocks and the messages between friends are run by Unity, on the Unity account described above.

Adding a friend

  • You can add someone with their friend code, from the “played with you” list, or from the results screen after a match.
  • Nobody becomes your friend unless you accept. A request you ignore stays just a request.
  • When you share your code, the game opens your phone's own share menu with a short message. That message contains your friend code, or a web link with your code in it. It never contains your cat name.
  • So that a code on its own (or a link) can find you, our server keeps one record matching your friend code to your Unity account number. Looking a code up needs the game, and each copy of the game can only look up a limited number of codes an hour, so codes cannot be guessed in bulk.
  • When you send someone a friend request, they see your cat name, your cat and your level on it, so they know who is asking. For that, our server keeps a small “player card” with just those three things. Only your friends and players you have sent a request to can read it — our server asks Unity who they are each time — and nobody else can look it up. Your friends' phones may also use it to show your name while you are offline.
  • Invited by a friend. If you came to the game through a friend's link or typed a friend's code, you and that friend each get a gift after your first finished match. When a friend's link sent you to Google Play to install the game, Google Play keeps the friend code from the link; in your first days the game asks Google Play, on your phone, for that note (the “install referrer”) and uses only the friend code in it. Our server then records that you were invited by that friend — the two Unity account numbers and the time, no names — so the gift is paid once.

What your friends see

While the game is open, it tells Unity what you are doing, and Unity passes that on to your friends only: whether you are online, on the menus or in a match (and which kind of match), your cat name, your cat, your level, and when you were last seen. Their game also receives your time-zone offset from UTC, which it does not show.

Messages between friends

There is no typed chat. Friends can send each other one of eight fixed phrases — LET'S PLAY!, GOOD GAME!, ONE MORE?, WAIT FOR ME, NICE!, SORRY!, THANKS! and BRB — and invitations to play together. Only the number of the phrase travels, never words, so nothing else can appear on anybody's screen. Only friends can send them.

You can remove a friend or block anyone. Blocking removes the friendship and stops every request, invitation and phrase from that player.

Friend alerts (Android)

On Android, the game can show a notification when a friend comes online: “Maya is online · Tap to play together”. It works like this.

  • When the game opens, it gets a notification address for this copy of the game on this phone from Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging, the service Android uses to deliver notifications. It sends that address to our server, with your Unity account number and your phone's time-zone setting (such as Europe/London), only while ALERTS is switched on and you have at least one friend. A phone with no friends, or with ALERTS off, sends no address and leaves no alert record on our server. Switching ALERTS off deletes the record at once.
  • When you open the game, our server asks Unity who your friends are. It does not keep that list. This happens whether or not your own ALERTS are on, so that friends who want alerts still get them. It sends each friend who does not have the game open a small message that contains only your Unity account number. Your name never passes through our server: your friend's phone shows the name it already knows for you from the friends list.
  • A friend is told about you at most once every three hours, and never between 10 at night and 8 in the morning in their own time zone. That is what the time-zone setting is for.
  • To make that work, the server keeps: for a player with ALERTS on and a friend, up to three notification addresses, the time zone, and when the game was last open; for each pair of friends, when the last alert was sent; and, for each player, when they last opened the game (so that it can happen at most once every ten minutes). How long each is kept is below.

To stop alerts, switch off ALERTS on the friends screen in the game, or turn off notifications for Meowtropolis in your phone's settings. On Android 13 and later the game asks for permission to show notifications when you make a friend, or when you switch ALERTS back on — never when the game first opens. If you say no, no alert is shown. There are no friend alerts on iPhone yet.

Ads (Android)

On Android, Meowtropolis has reward videos: a short ad you choose to watch in return for something in the game. There are no other ads — no banners, no ads that open by themselves, never an ad during a match and never one when the game starts. A video plays only when you press its button, in one of three places:

  • the free gift, in the store and behind the video button on the home screen (a few a day);
  • double the coins a match paid, on the results screen;
  • double the daily gift.

The videos come from Google AdMob, Google's ad service. There are no ads on iPhone.

Never personalised

Every request the game makes for a video tells Google to treat the player as a child and as under the age of consent, asks only for ads rated G (suitable for everyone), and switches personalised ads off. That is the same for every player, whatever age they gave. With these settings Google does not pick ads from what it knows about you and does not follow you with ads from other apps or websites. The permission that lets an app read the phone's advertising ID is removed from the game when it is built, so the ad library cannot read it.

What Google's ad library collects

The ad library is Google's own code running inside the game. To fetch, show and count videos, and to stop fraud, it sends Google: your phone's internet (IP) address; what happened with each ad (loaded, shown, finished, tapped); diagnostic information about how the ad library is running; and an app set ID — a number Android gives to the apps of one developer on one phone, which cannot be used to follow you into other companies' apps. On most phones the game loads one video in the background once the home screen appears, so it is ready when you press; that loading already sends Google these things, even if you never watch. Google handles this under its rules for apps that use its services and Google's privacy policy.

Checking that a video was watched

So that a changed copy of the game cannot collect rewards for videos it never played, when a video starts the game gives Google two things to pass on to our server: your Unity account number and a random code used for that one video. When you earn the reward, Google sends both to our server in a message only Google can sign, and the server pays the reward only for a video Google has confirmed. Each confirmation is kept for 30 days, then deleted.

Tapping an ad can open a web page or a store page outside the game. The ads are made by other companies and chosen by Google; if you ever see one that does not belong in a game children play, please tell us.

Signing in with Google Play Games

On Android, the game quietly asks Google Play Games who you are when it starts. This exists so that the things you own — especially coins you paid for — can survive a new phone instead of living only on this one.

Google gives the game a short one-time code, which the game passes straight to Unity so Unity can link its account to your Play Games account. The game itself never receives or stores your Google name, your email address or your Google ID.

If you have no Play Games account, or you say no, or you are offline, nothing bad happens: the game carries on exactly as before. The only thing you do not get is the ability to recover your progress on a new phone.

Buying coins

Coins can be earned by playing, and they can also be bought with real money. Buying is handled entirely by Google Play. The game never sees your card, your billing address or any payment detail — it only hears back from Google whether the purchase succeeded.

The receipt Google returns (the order number and purchase token) is kept on your phone until the purchase is settled, and it is sent to our server, which asks Google whether the purchase is real before giving you what you bought. The server keeps one record of each purchase — a scrambled form of the purchase token, which pack it was, the order number and the time — so that one purchase can never be paid out twice. It never holds a card, a name or any payment detail.

So that a purchase can be recognised as yours if you change phone, the game gives Google a scrambled, one-way version of your player number. It cannot be turned back into anything.

Apart from that check, what leaves your phone about a purchase is the line in the usage records above: which pack, what it gave you, and your new balance. No price and no payment details.

What stays on your phone and never leaves

  • Your progress: coins, the cats and skills you own, your records, your rank, your settings.
  • Your purchase receipts (each is also sent once to our server to be checked, as described above).
  • Your “played with you” list, the friend requests you have sent, and the names of your friends that your phone remembers so a friend alert can say who is online. Your phone also keeps a copy of the player cards (cat name, cat, level) of your friends and of the players who sent you a request.
  • Reminders. If you allow notifications, the daily reminders are scheduled by your phone itself — nothing is sent to us or to anyone else in order to send them. (Friend alerts are different; they are explained above.)

All of it is deleted when you uninstall the game.

Where the data is stored, and who can read it

Match results, leaderboard rows, names and usage records are stored in a small Google Firebase database in Europe (Belgium), reached through a program written for this game alone. The friend-alert records, the friend-code records and player cards, your coins and what you own, the purchase checks, the invite gifts, the video confirmations and deletion requests are kept apart from those, in a second database of their own, also in Europe, reached through a second program written for this game.

  • Only Ben can read them, through his own Google account. No other app and no other person has access to the databases, and the game itself has no key to them.
  • The usage records cannot be read over the internet at all. There is deliberately no way to request them; that ability was never built.
  • Leaderboard rows are meant to be seen: names, cats and scores are shown to other players inside the game. Player numbers are never shown to anyone.
  • Nothing is sold. Our servers give nothing to advertisers, data brokers or anybody else. (What Google's ad library collects by itself, on your phone, is described under Ads.)

Two other companies are unavoidably involved, each under their own privacy policy: Google (which hosts the databases and our servers, runs Play Games sign-in, takes the payments, delivers friend alerts on Android, tells the game which friend's link it was installed from, and shows the reward videos through AdMob) and Unity (which provides the game's account, the copy of your save, the rooms and the connection for live matches, and the friends list, friend status and messages between friends). You can read Google's privacy policy and Unity's player privacy policy. As with any internet connection, their systems see your device's internet address when your phone talks to them; the game itself does not record or keep it. The Unity engine's own hardware statistics and engine diagnostics are switched off, so the engine does not report on your phone to Unity by itself.

How long it is kept

Being honest about this: the game has no automatic deletion schedule. Nothing is set up to erase data after a certain number of days. What actually happens is:

  • Only your five most recent match recordings are kept. A sixth match overwrites the oldest one.
  • Your leaderboard row and your duel row are single rows that are rewritten each time you play, not a history.
  • The usage records build up, and stay until they are deleted by hand.
  • The friend-alert record is deleted when you switch ALERTS off, and automatically when the game has not been open for 60 days. It keeps at most three notification addresses; an address that Google reports as no longer working is removed. The record of when each pair of friends was last alerted, and of when a player last opened the game, are deleted automatically after one day. Your friend-code record stays until you delete your account.
  • Your player card (cat name, cat, level) is refreshed each time you open the game and deleted automatically when the game has not been open for 30 days, or when you delete your account.
  • Your coins and what you own, and the record of a friend's invite, stay until you delete your account.
  • The confirmation that a reward video was watched is deleted after 30 days, or when you delete your account.
  • The record of each real-money purchase (a scrambled purchase token, the pack, the order number and the time) is kept for good, because it is what stops one purchase being paid out twice. When you delete your account, everything linking it to you is removed from it.
  • Your friends list and the copy of your save stay with Unity until you remove a friend or delete your account. How long Unity keeps its own records beyond that is set by Unity.
  • What Google's ad library sends Google is kept by Google, under Google's own policies.
  • When you delete your account, all of the above is deleted 7 days after you ask (see below). A request to delete from the web page that does not match any account is kept for up to 30 days so we can answer it, then deleted.

So: your data stays until you delete your account, or until it is cleared out as described above. If that changes, this page will say so.

Children, and a note for parents

Meowtropolis is a cat game for everyone, and children play it. It was built with that in mind, and this section is written for parents.

What the game collects from a child

The same as from any other player, as described on this page, and nothing more: the random player number and the Unity account number; the age answer; a cat name; the results of matches; your child's coins and what they own; the usage records (which include the country the phone is set to and the kind of phone); and, only if they are used, friends, friend alerts, invites and purchases. The game never asks a child for a real name, an email address, a phone number, a photo, their voice or their location, and there is no way to type a message to another player.

All of it is used only to run the game: to keep progress, run leaderboards and live matches, keep friends lists, stop cheating, and learn which parts of the game work. None of it is sold, and none of it is used for advertising.

Ads

  • Only reward videos, on Android, and only when the child presses the button for one. Never in a match and never by themselves.
  • Every ad request, for every player of every age, is marked as directed at children and under the age of consent, and asks only for ads rated G. Google does not personalise these ads or use them to follow anyone around other apps, and the game cannot read the advertising ID.
  • Google's ad library still receives the phone's internet address, what happened with the ad, diagnostics and an app set ID, to show and count the ads (see Ads).

How the game protects children

  • Under 13, the chosen name stays among friends. A player who says they are under 13 is shown to everyone else under a nickname the game makes up; only friends they have accepted see the name they chose.
  • There is no typed chat. Players cannot send each other any text they wrote, ever. Friends can send only eight fixed phrases, such as GOOD GAME!.
  • Only friends can reach you, and only after you accept them. Someone you played with can send a friend request; it does nothing unless it is accepted. Friends can see when you are online and invite you to play. You can block anyone.
  • Nothing personal is asked for. No name, no email, no location — only how old the player is, to decide how their name is shown.
  • Names are filtered for rude and hateful words before they can appear on anybody else's screen.
  • Friend links and shared friend codes never contain the child's cat name.
  • Friend alerts are never sent between 10 at night and 8 in the morning, and they can be switched off in the game.

Parents should also know that the game sells packs for real money, between roughly one and twenty US dollars. Please use your phone's or Google Play's parental controls and password protection if a child plays on your device.

What parents can do

  • See what we hold. Write to bendabas1@gmail.com with your child's cat name and friend code (the code is on the friends screen), and Ben will tell you what our servers hold about that account.
  • Have it deleted. Use DELETE ACCOUNT in the game's Settings, the deletion page, or write to the same address. See below for exactly what goes.
  • Stop any more being collected. Delete the account and uninstall the game; nothing more is sent after that. Friend alerts can be switched off on their own with ALERTS on the friends screen.
  • Ask anything. The game is made by one person, and he answers these himself.

How to delete your account and your data

In the game: Settings → YOUR ACCOUNT → DELETE ACCOUNT, then hold the red button. Your account is deleted 7 days later. Until then the game shows a notice each time it opens, with a button to keep your account, in case you change your mind or someone else pressed it; the same notice has DELETE NOW if you do not want to wait.

Without the game: use the account deletion page. It asks for your friend code and your cat name, because together they are how we find your account. A request from the web is carried out after the same 7 days, and whoever still plays that account can keep it from inside the game during that time. Or write to bendabas1@gmail.com with your cat name and, if you used Friends, your friend code.

Deleting your account removes: your progress and coins (including bought coins) on the phone and in the cloud copy of your save; your leaderboard rows, your duel row, your stored match recordings and your claimed name; your usage records; your friend-alert record, your friend code and your player card; the server's count of your coins and what you own; your invite records; your reward-video confirmations; your friends list, friend requests and blocks; and your game account with Unity. Coins are not refunded.

If you delete your account in the game, all of this goes at once when the 7 days are up (or when you press DELETE NOW). If you ask from the web page and never open the game again, everything on our own servers goes when the 7 days are up; your account with Unity (sign-in, friends list and the cloud copy of your save) goes at the same time or, at the latest, within 30 days.

You can remove friends and block players yourself at any time, inside the game.

What deleting your account does not remove: Google Play's record of anything you bought, and the Google Play Games link if you signed in with it (you can delete that at play.google.com/games/profile); routine request logs kept by our hosting provider, which are deleted automatically within 30 days; the record of each real-money purchase, which stays with nothing left in it that points to you (the pack, the order number, the time and a scrambled purchase token); what Google's ad library sent Google, which Google keeps under its own policies; and your cat name in the “played with you” list on the phones of people you played live with, which we cannot reach.
