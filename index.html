Meowtropolis

Privacy Policy

In effect from 25 September 2026

The short version

  • You never make an account, and the game never asks for your name, your email or your phone number.
  • The game gives your save file a random number when you first open it. That number is how the leaderboard tells one player from another. It is not your phone, and it is not you.
  • You get a cat name such as SleepyBiscuit. You can change it. Other players can see it, so please do not put your real name in it.
  • When you finish a match, the game sends the result of that match so it can appear on the leaderboard and so other people can play against a recording of your run.
  • You can add friends. A friend sees when you are online and can invite you to play. There is no typed chat: friends can only send each other a few fixed phrases, such as GOOD GAME!. On Android, the game can tell you when a friend comes online.
  • There are no ads and no advertising companies in this game. Nothing you do here is sold, shared for marketing, or used to follow you around other apps.
  • If you delete the game, the random number on your phone is gone with it. If you also want your rows removed from the leaderboard, write to us and we will delete them.

Who wrote this

Meowtropolis is made by one person, Ben Dabas. The whole game, the leaderboard and the small servers behind it are his. There is no company, no marketing team, and nobody else with access. You can always reach him at bendabas1@gmail.com.

What Meowtropolis never collects

This is not a promise about how we behave. These things simply are not in the game — there is no code anywhere that asks for them.

  • No name, email address or phone number. There is no sign-up form and no login screen.
  • No advertising ID and no hardware ID. Nothing lets anyone follow you into another app. (On Android, friend alerts need an address that lets a notification reach this game on your phone. That is explained below.)
  • No location. The game has no permission to know where you are and never asks for it. It does read your phone's time-zone setting; here is why.
  • No contacts, photos, camera, microphone or files. Your friends in the game are people you added inside the game, not your phone's contacts.
  • No ads, and no advertising or tracking companies at all. There is no ad network, no attribution service and no outside analytics service in the game.
  • No typed chat. Players cannot write text to each other. Friends can send one of eight fixed phrases, and nothing else.
  • No card numbers or payment details. If you buy coins, Google handles the payment. The game never sees the card.
  • No age or date of birth. The game never asks.

Who you are, inside the game

Your player number

The first time you open Meowtropolis, the game writes a long random number into its save file on your phone. Every match result the game sends carries that number, so the leaderboard knows that today's run and last week's run belong to the same player.

It is a fresh random number, not anything taken from your phone. Nobody can work backwards from it to a person, a device or an account. Deleting the game deletes the save file, and the link is genuinely broken — a new install starts again as somebody new.

One exception, and it is your choice: if you use the feature that links two of your own phones, you type the same short code on both, and that code becomes the player number on both of them.

Your friend code

To make friends possible, the game also gives you a friend code: eight random letters and numbers, such as K7M2QX9A. Like your player number, it is made up by the game and is not taken from you or your phone. It is what you give a friend so they can add you.

Friends are keyed to the account Unity gives your copy of the game (see playing against other people), not to your player number. That account number is never shown to anyone.

Your cat name

The game gives you a two-word cat name from a fixed list of cat words — SleepyBiscuit, GrumpyWaffle, and so on. You can roll a new one, or type your own instead.

Your name is public inside the game. It is printed next to your score on the leaderboards, it is shown to other players when they meet a recording of one of your runs, it is shown to the people you play live matches with, and your friends see it on their friends list. Please treat it as something the world can read, and do not type your real name, your school, or anything you would not want a stranger to see.

A typed name has to be 3 to 14 letters or digits, with no spaces or symbols, and it is checked against a list of rude and hateful words on both the phone and the server. That check catches the obvious ones. It cannot catch everything, so if you ever see a name that should not be there, please tell us and it will be removed.

What the game sends when you play

After a match

When a match finishes, the game sends one small record of that match to our own server. It contains:

  • your player number and your cat name;
  • which map you played and which cat you wore;
  • twenty-five numbers describing how big your cat grew during the match;
  • which skills you had equipped and how many times you used each one;
  • a few numbers describing how you played — how much you chased, how far you roamed, how eagerly you used skills;
  • your rank badge, your total wins and your rating on that map.

Those last numbers exist for one reason: they let the game replay your run as an opponent for somebody else. When you play the online mode, some of the cats around you can be recordings of other people's matches, played back. Yours can become one of them.

The leaderboards

From the same match, the server keeps your best result on that map — your name, your cat, your score, your rating, your wins and your rank badge — so the leaderboard can be ordered. After a 1 v 1 duel, the game also sends your duel rating, your wins, your losses and your best winning streak, for the duel ladder.

When the game asks the server for a leaderboard, it sends your player number so that the server can point at your own row and tell you your position. The server never sends anybody's player number back to anybody. All you ever see about another player is the name, the cat and the numbers on their row.

How the game is used

Meowtropolis records what happens in the game itself, so it can be made better: which maps people stop choosing, which skills nobody buys, where in the tutorial people give up. These records carry your player number, and nothing else about you. There is no free text of any kind in them. They are:

WhenWhat is recorded
You open the gamethe version of the game, how many matches you have played, how many coins you have
A match startsthe map, the cat, the skills you equipped, and whether it is an online match or a bot match
A match endsthe map, the cat, the skills, your final size, your tier, your place, how many rivals, how many cats you ate, coins and stars earned, how many seconds it lasted, and how it ended
You quit mid‑matchthe map, the cat, your size and how many seconds in you left
You spend coinswhat you bought (a cat, a skill, a skill level) and how many coins you had afterwards
You collect a rewardwhich daily gift, mission or achievement, and the coins it paid
Tutorialwhich tutorial step you finished
You tap a reminderwhich reminder it was
You use an item in a matchwhich item
An online match endswhat kind of phone it ran on (the model name, the system version, the graphics chip, the amount of memory and the number of processor cores), how smoothly the match ran and how steady the connection was

Times are recorded only to the nearest whole second, and nothing here records where you are, what else is on your phone, or anything you typed. The phone details describe the kind of phone, so we can tell which phones the game runs badly on. They contain no serial number and no device ID.

Playing live against other people

The live modes — 1 v 1, the online city match, Catball and Cats vs Dogs — connect phones through Unity, the company whose software the game is built with. Unity provides the room where players meet and the connection their phones talk over.

Unity also gives every copy of the game an account of its own when the game starts, so that its services will answer. It has no name, email or phone number on it. It has a number, and it holds your friend code and your friends list (see Friends).

What the phones in a match tell each other is: your cat name, which cat you are wearing, your skills and their levels, your rating or rank, where your cat is moving and what it has eaten. In the online city match, the room also holds a number saying how well your phone can run the match, so the strongest phone can host it. Your player number is not sent. The other phones in the room can see your Unity account number, because that is how Unity's rooms work, and it is never shown to anyone.

To find players who are near you first, a waiting room shows other phones looking for a match how many hours ahead of or behind UTC the phone that opened it is — for example, “three hours ahead”. That is the time-zone setting on the phone, never your position.

After a live match, each player's game keeps a short “played with you” list on their own phone, the last 25 players, with their cat names and cats, so they can send a friend request. So the people you played with keep your cat name in that list, and you keep theirs. A friend request does nothing until it is accepted.

Friends

The friends list, friend requests, blocks and the messages between friends are run by Unity, on the Unity account described above.

Adding a friend

  • You can add someone with their friend code, from the “played with you” list, or from the results screen after a match.
  • Nobody becomes your friend unless you accept. A request you ignore stays just a request.
  • When you share your code, the game opens your phone's own share menu with a short message. That message contains your friend code, or a web link with your code in it. It never contains your cat name.
  • So that a code on its own (or a link) can find you, our server keeps one record matching your friend code to your Unity account number. Looking a code up needs the game, and each copy of the game can only look up a limited number of codes an hour, so codes cannot be guessed in bulk.

What your friends see

While the game is open, it tells Unity what you are doing, and Unity passes that on to your friends only: whether you are online, on the menus or in a match (and which kind of match), your cat name, your cat, your level, and when you were last seen. Their game also receives your time-zone offset from UTC, which it does not show.

Messages between friends

There is no typed chat. Friends can send each other one of eight fixed phrases — LET'S PLAY!, GOOD GAME!, ONE MORE?, WAIT FOR ME, NICE!, SORRY!, THANKS! and BRB — and invitations to play together. Only the number of the phrase travels, never words, so nothing else can appear on anybody's screen. Only friends can send them.

You can remove a friend or block anyone. Blocking removes the friendship and stops every request, invitation and phrase from that player.

Friend alerts (Android)

On Android, the game can show a notification when a friend comes online: “Maya is online · Tap to play together”. It works like this.

  • When the game opens, it gets a notification address for this copy of the game on this phone from Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging, the service Android uses to deliver notifications. It sends that address to our server with your Unity account number, your phone's time-zone setting (such as Europe/London), and whether alerts are switched on. This happens on every Android phone, and it also happens when alerts are switched off, so that the server knows not to send any.
  • When you open the game, our server asks Unity who your friends are. It does not keep that list. It sends each friend who does not have the game open a small message that contains only your Unity account number. Your name never passes through our server: your friend's phone shows the name it already knows for you from the friends list.
  • A friend is told about you at most once every three hours, and never between 10 at night and 8 in the morning in their own time zone. That is what the time-zone setting is for.
  • To make that work, the server keeps: up to three notification addresses per player, the time zone, whether alerts are on, when the game was last open, and, for each pair of friends, when the last alert was sent.

To stop alerts, switch off ALERTS on the friends screen in the game, or turn off notifications for Meowtropolis in your phone's settings. On Android 13 and later the game asks for permission to show notifications when you make a friend, or when you switch ALERTS back on — never when the game first opens. If you say no, no alert is shown. There are no friend alerts on iPhone yet.

Signing in with Google Play Games

On Android, the game quietly asks Google Play Games who you are when it starts. This exists so that the things you own — especially coins you paid for — can survive a new phone instead of living only on this one.

Google gives the game a short one-time code, which the game passes straight to Unity so Unity can link its account to your Play Games account. The game itself never receives or stores your Google name, your email address or your Google ID.

If you have no Play Games account, or you say no, or you are offline, nothing bad happens: the game carries on exactly as before. The only thing you do not get is the ability to recover your progress on a new phone.

Buying coins

Coins can be earned by playing, and they can also be bought with real money. Buying is handled entirely by Google Play. The game never sees your card, your billing address or any payment detail — it only hears back from Google whether the purchase succeeded.

The receipt Google returns (the order number and purchase token) is stored only on your own phone, so the coins can be given to you exactly once even if the app closes at the wrong moment. It is not uploaded anywhere.

So that a purchase can be recognised as yours if you change phone, the game gives Google a scrambled, one-way version of your player number. It cannot be turned back into anything.

The only thing that leaves your phone about a purchase is one line in the usage records above: which coin pack, how many coins, and your new balance. No price, no receipt, no payment details.

What stays on your phone and never leaves

  • Your progress: coins, the cats and skills you own, your records, your rank, your settings.
  • Your purchase receipts.
  • Your “played with you” list, the friend requests you have sent, and the names of your friends that your phone remembers so a friend alert can say who is online.
  • Reminders. If you allow notifications, the daily reminders are scheduled by your phone itself — nothing is sent to us or to anyone else in order to send them. (Friend alerts are different; they are explained above.)

All of it is deleted when you uninstall the game.

Where the data is stored, and who can read it

Match results, leaderboard rows, names and usage records are stored in a small Google Firebase database in Europe (Belgium), reached through a program written for this game alone. The friend-alert records and the friend-code records are kept apart from those, in a second database of their own, also in Europe, reached through a second program written for this game.

  • Only Ben can read them, through his own Google account. No other app and no other person has access to the databases, and the game itself has no key to them.
  • The usage records cannot be read over the internet at all. There is deliberately no way to request them; that ability was never built.
  • Leaderboard rows are meant to be seen: names, cats and scores are shown to other players inside the game. Player numbers are never shown to anyone.
  • Nothing is sold. Nothing is given to advertisers, data brokers or anybody else.

Two other companies are unavoidably involved, each under their own privacy policy: Google (which hosts the databases, runs Play Games sign-in, takes the payments and delivers friend alerts on Android) and Unity (which provides the game's account, the connection for live matches, and the friends list, friend status and messages between friends). You can read Google's privacy policy and Unity's player privacy policy. As with any internet connection, their systems see your device's internet address when your phone talks to them; the game itself does not record or keep it.

How long it is kept

Being honest about this: the game has no automatic deletion schedule. Nothing is set up to erase data after a certain number of days. What actually happens is:

  • Only your five most recent match recordings are kept. A sixth match overwrites the oldest one.
  • Your leaderboard row and your duel row are single rows that are rewritten each time you play, not a history.
  • The usage records build up, and stay until they are deleted by hand.
  • The friend-alert record keeps at most three notification addresses per player; an address that Google reports as no longer working is removed. The rest of it, the record of when each pair of friends was last alerted, and your friend-code record stay until they are deleted by hand.
  • Your friends list stays with Unity until you remove a friend. How long Unity keeps its own records is set by Unity.

So: your data stays until you ask for it to be deleted, or until it is cleared out. If that changes, this page will say so.

Children

Meowtropolis is a cat game and children play it. It was built with that in mind:

  • There is no typed chat. Players cannot send each other any text they wrote, ever. Friends can send only eight fixed phrases, such as GOOD GAME!.
  • Only friends can reach you, and only after you accept them. Someone you played with can send a friend request; it does nothing unless it is accepted. Friends can see when you are online and invite you to play. You can block anyone.
  • Nothing personal is asked for. No name, no age, no email, no location.
  • Names are filtered for rude and hateful words before they can appear on anybody else's screen.
  • Friend links and shared friend codes never contain the child's cat name.
  • Friend alerts are never sent between 10 at night and 8 in the morning, and they can be switched off in the game.

The one thing parents should know is that the game sells coins for real money, between roughly one and twenty US dollars. Please use your phone's or Google Play's parental controls and password protection if a child plays on your device.

The game does not ask anyone's age, so we cannot tell who is a child. If you are a parent and you want everything connected to your child's game removed, write to us and we will delete it. See below.

How to have your data deleted

Write to bendabas1@gmail.com with your cat name — the name shown on your profile screen in the game — and say you want your data deleted.

Your name is what lets us find your rows, so please copy it exactly. If you have used Friends, please also send your friend code from the friends screen; that is what lets us find your friend-alert and friend-code records. We will delete your leaderboard rows, your duel row, your stored match recordings, your claimed name, your usage records, your friend-alert record and your friend-code record. It will normally be done within 30 days, and you will get a reply saying it is done.

You can remove friends and block players yourself at any time, inside the game.

You can also delete everything on your side at any time by uninstalling the game, which removes the save file, your progress, the random player number and the “played with you” list on your phone. That does not remove rows already on the leaderboard or your friend-alert record — for those, write to us.

Google and Unity hold their own records under their own policies; requests about those go to them. That includes the friends list, friend status and friend messages, which Unity holds. For anything you bought, Google Play holds the purchase history.

That includes advertising. Everything on this page about ads describes Meowtropolis as it is today: there is no ad network in the game and no advertising company receives anything. If that ever changes, it will not change quietly — this page will say so before the version containing ads is released, the date at the top will change, and what Google Play shows you about this game's data will change with it.
